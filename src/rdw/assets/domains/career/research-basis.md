# Research Basis for Professional Outreach

Checked 2026-08-02. These sources inform defaults; they do not guarantee a response and should be refreshed when platform behavior or evidence changes.

- LinkedIn's current help says a connection invitation should be short, thoughtful, and specific, explaining who the sender is and why they are reaching out: https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a563153
- LinkedIn's InMail guidance recommends a concise goal, recipient-centered relevance, a clear action, visible research, and roughly 100 words or fewer for an introductory message: https://www.linkedin.com/business/sales/blog/inmail/what-a-strong-linkedin-inmail-template-looks-like
- LinkedIn's recruiter dataset reports higher response for shorter messages and materially higher acceptance for personalized InMail; it defines useful personalization as a specific accomplishment, work item, or shared context rather than a generic compliment: https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/tips-for-writing-inmails-from-linkedin-recruiters
- LinkedIn's 2025 AI-assisted messaging guidance still requires editing for the sender's voice and specific context; automated drafting is an editable starting point, not final approval: https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/ai-assisted-messaging-recruiter

RDW therefore gates career outreach on recipient-specific evidence, candidate proof, brevity, a clear low-friction CTA, content-bound provenance, and human review. It does not encode unsupported claims about a universally optimal template.
